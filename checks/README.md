# Checks

One script per part, `partNN_checks.py`, that recomputes every number the post quotes and fails loudly if any differs from the text.

Part 0 quotes no derived numbers except the figure's end points, which `figures/part00_sharpen_vs_expand.py` prints when it runs.

`part01_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 1, including the worked examples, the check-yourself answers, the seeded simulation behind the pass@k animation, and an exact and Monte Carlo check that the pass@k estimator is unbiased. It re-reads the experiment numbers from a pinned commit of sharpen-or-expand and fails if any value differs from the text.

`part02_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 2: the worked gradient step and the numbers shown in the reshaping animation, the compounding and length examples, the loss-normalization weights, the toy exposure-bias model behind the figure, and the check-yourself answers. It checks the softmax cross-entropy gradient against finite differences, the identity $H(q, \pi) = H(q) + \mathrm{KL}(q \Vert \pi)$ on random distributions, and $\nabla \log p_\theta(x) = \mathbb{E}_{q}[\nabla \log \pi_\theta]$ on a small autoregressive model. It confirms which SFT checkpoint the experiment reports evaluate and re-reads every experiment number from a pinned commit of sharpen-or-expand.

`part03_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 3: the Bradley–Terry and β examples, the binary-reward tilt and the figure's marked points, the ratio-of-ratios and likelihood-displacement examples, the gradient weights, the length example, the check-yourself answers, and every number printed by the two animations (the opening DPO picture and the tilt-and-displacement animation). It checks numerically that the Gibbs policy maximizes the KL-regularized objective (against random and perturbed alternatives, with value $\beta \log Z$), that $Z$ cancels in Bradley–Terry, that the DPO gradient matches finite differences on a small autoregressive model and that shared-prefix tokens cancel, that the 0/1-reward optimum multiplies the odds of success by $e^{1/\beta}$ while leaving $q(\tau \mid x)$ and $N_{\text{eff}}$ unchanged, and that one DPO step with free logits always raises the preferred response while the shared-hidden-state toy lowers both. It re-reads the DPO design statements from a pinned commit of sharpen-or-expand.

`part04_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 4: the seeded two-problem simulation behind the opening animation (and every number the animation prints), the variance example at $p = 0.01$, the baseline-noise figure, the sequence-ratio, PPO-clipping, entropy and sparse-reward examples, and the check-yourself answers. It checks numerically that the policy gradient $\mathbb{E}[R\, \nabla \log \pi_\theta(\tau)]$ matches finite differences on a small autoregressive model, that $\nabla J = p_\theta(x)\, \mathbb{E}_q[\nabla \log \pi_\theta]$ for a 0/1 reward, that a constant baseline leaves the gradient unchanged, that a group-mean baseline rescales it by $(G-1)/G$ while leave-one-out is exact, that PPO's clipped objective has the stated gradient in all four cases and reduces to the policy gradient at ratio 1, that a clipped token can still be moved by updates on other tokens, and that the k3 KL estimator is unbiased and non-negative. It re-reads the RL design statements from a pinned commit of sharpen-or-expand.

`part05_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 5: the seeded group simulation behind the opening animation (and every number the animation prints), the closed-form advantages for 0/1 rewards, the population and Bessel-corrected values, the per-prompt weights in the figure, the mixed-group and at-least-one-success probabilities, and the check-yourself answers. It checks the closed forms against direct computation for every $k$ at $G = 4, 8, 16$; checks by exact enumeration of every group that mean-centering gives $(G-1)/G$ times the policy gradient while dividing by the group standard deviation gives $w_G(p)\, \nabla p$ with a weight that depends on $p$; and checks the large-$G$ limit $1/\sqrt{p(1-p)}$, the derivative of $2\arcsin\sqrt{p}$. It re-reads verl 0.9.1's GRPO advantage function and the RL design statements from pinned commits.


`part06_checks.py` recomputes every number in Part 6: the seeded twelve-prompt simulation behind the opening animation (every number the animation prints; the animation reproduces it draw by draw) and the seed search it discloses, the exact expected regime shares of that batch by enumeration and by a 3,000-seed Monte Carlo run, the dynamic-sampling cost and the resampling bound $1 - (1 - p)^{Gk}$ (checked by simulation), the standard-deviation and pass@8 weights, the per-token weights of verl 0.9.1's `agg_loss` reimplemented on Part 2's two-response batch (with the source lines asserted at the pinned commit), the length toy (exact drifts, the figure's trajectory, Monte Carlo checks of token-mean and the shared-stop case), the clipping table, Clip-Higher and compounding, and the design statements and step-1 rollout counts of the experiments box, re-read from sharpen-or-expand at a pinned commit.
