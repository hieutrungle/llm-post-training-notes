# Block 4 — Policy Gradients, REINFORCE, PPO, KL, and Entropy

## 1. RL objective for an LLM

For prompt \(x\), a policy generates trajectory

\[
\tau=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)
\]

with

\[
\pi_\theta(\tau|x)
=
\prod_t\pi_\theta(y_t|x,y_{<t}).
\]

Given reward \(R(x,\tau)\),

\[
\boxed{
J(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{x}
\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}[R(x,\tau)].
}
\]

For binary verifier reward,

\[
J(\theta)=P_\theta(\text{success}).
\]

---

## 2. Policy-gradient derivation

Write

\[
J(\theta)
=
\sum_\tau
\pi_\theta(\tau)R(\tau).
\]

Differentiate:

\[
\nabla_\theta J
=
\sum_\tau
\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(\tau)R(\tau).
\]

Use

\[
\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(\tau)
=
\pi_\theta(\tau)
\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_\theta J
=
\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}
[
R(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau)
].
}
\]

For autoregressive LMs:

\[
\log\pi_\theta(\tau)
=
\sum_t
\log\pi_\theta(y_t|x,y_{<t}),
\]

so

\[
\nabla_\theta J
=
\mathbb E
\left[
R(\tau)
\sum_t
\nabla_\theta
\log\pi_\theta(y_t|x,y_{<t})
\right].
\]

---

## 3. Why the log-derivative trick matters

Token sampling is discrete.

We cannot ordinarily backpropagate through the sampled token identity.

The log-derivative trick moves the derivative onto the log probability of the sampled behavior.

The mental model:

\[
\boxed{
\text{sample}
\rightarrow
\text{evaluate}
\rightarrow
\text{increase probability of high-reward behavior}.
}
\]

---

## 4. Credit assignment

With terminal reward, a 2,000-token correct trajectory may give the same sequence-level positive signal to every token.

The optimizer does not inherently know which token contained the critical reasoning insight.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{sequence-level reward gives coarse token-level credit}.
}
\]

---

## 5. Why REINFORCE has high variance

If success probability is \(p=0.01\), a batch of 100 trajectories may contain:
- 0 successes,
- 1 success,
- 3 successes, etc.

The gradient can vary dramatically across batches.

This motivates baselines.

---

## 6. Baselines

Replace reward by

\[
R-b.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\nabla J
=
E[(R-b)\nabla\log\pi].
}
\]

If the baseline does not depend improperly on the sampled action,

\[
E[b\nabla\log\pi]=0.
\]

Proof:

\[
E[b\nabla\log\pi]
=
b\sum_a\pi(a|s)\nabla\log\pi(a|s)
\]

\[
=
b\sum_a\nabla\pi(a|s)
=
b\nabla1
=
0.
\]

So the baseline can reduce variance without biasing the expected gradient.

---

## 7. Advantage

Define

\[
\boxed{
A=R-b.
}
\]

Then:
- \(A>0\): increase probability,
- \(A<0\): decrease probability,
- \(A\approx0\): little update.

In standard RL:

\[
A^\pi(s,a)
=
Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s).
\]

---

## 8. Importance sampling

Suppose data were generated by

\[
\pi_{\text{old}}
\]

but we are optimizing

\[
\pi_\theta.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
r_t(\theta)
=
\frac{
\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)
}{
\pi_{\text{old}}(a_t|s_t)
}.
}
\]

This corrects for policy change between rollout generation and optimization.

---

## 9. Long-sequence importance-ratio instability

For an entire trajectory:

\[
\frac{\pi_\theta(\tau)}
{\pi_{\text{old}}(\tau)}
=
\prod_t
\frac{\pi_\theta(y_t|s_t)}
{\pi_{\text{old}}(y_t|s_t)}.
\]

Even a small token ratio such as \(1.02\) compounds:

\[
1.02^{500}
\approx19956.
\]

This motivates conservative updates.

---

## 10. PPO clipped objective

PPO uses

\[
\boxed{
L^{\text{CLIP}}
=
E_t
\left[
\min
\left(
r_tA_t,
\operatorname{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t
\right)
\right].
}
\]

For positive advantage:
- increasing probability helps,
- but beyond roughly \(1+\epsilon\), extra incentive saturates.

For negative advantage:
- decreasing probability helps,
- but below roughly \(1-\epsilon\), extra incentive saturates.

Important:

\[
\boxed{
\text{clipping limits objective incentive, not the actual final policy ratio}.
}
\]

---

## 11. Three policies

### Current policy

\[
\pi_\theta
\]

the model currently being optimized.

### Old policy

\[
\pi_{\text{old}}
\]

the policy that generated rollout data.

Appears in:

\[
\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{old}}.
\]

### Reference policy

\[
\pi_{\text{ref}}
\]

a frozen anchor, often the SFT model.

Appears in:

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}}).
\]

Conceptually:

\[
\boxed{
\pi_{\text{old}}
\text{ controls local update stability}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\pi_{\text{ref}}
\text{ controls long-term policy drift}.
}
\]

---

## 12. KL regularization

A common objective is

\[
J
=
E[R]
-
\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}).
\]

KL can:
- improve stability,
- preserve broad behavior,
- reduce reward hacking,

but it may also resist movement into rare/novel regions.

This creates a tension:

\[
\boxed{
\text{stability}
\leftrightarrow
\text{exploration}.
}
\]

---

## 13. Entropy

Token entropy is

\[
\boxed{
H(\pi(\cdot|s))
=
-\sum_a\pi(a|s)\log\pi(a|s).
}
\]

Higher entropy means more spread-out token probabilities.

Adding

\[
+\alpha H(\pi)
\]

encourages exploration.

But:

\[
\boxed{
\text{token entropy}\neq\text{semantic reasoning diversity}.
}
\]

---

## 14. Exploration versus exploitation

Exploitation:

\[
\pi(\tau_{\text{known good}})\uparrow.
\]

Exploration:

maintain enough probability on alternatives to discover new successful behavior.

Too much exploitation can cause early mode collapse.

Too much exploration can reduce reward and stability.

---

## 15. Sparse rewards and the expansion challenge

If

\[
p_{\text{success}}=10^{-4},
\]

almost every rollout gets reward zero.

RL cannot reinforce a successful region it never samples.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{on-policy RL can amplify rare successes,
but may struggle with effectively invisible ones}.
}
\]

This is central to sharpen vs expand.

# Research Checkpoint — Questions and Answers

## Q1

Derive

\[
\nabla_\theta E_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]
=
E[
R(\tau)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau)
].
\]

Why is the log-derivative trick necessary?

### Answer

Start with

\[
J=\sum_\tau\pi_\theta(\tau)R(\tau)
\]

and differentiate:

\[
\nabla J
=
\sum_\tau\nabla\pi_\theta(\tau)R(\tau).
\]

Use

\[
\nabla\pi=\pi\nabla\log\pi
\]

to obtain the expectation form.

The trick is needed because discrete token sampling is not ordinarily differentiable; we differentiate the log probability of sampled behavior instead.

---

## Q2

Why can we subtract a baseline without biasing the expected gradient?

### Answer

Because for an action-independent baseline,

\[
E[b\nabla\log\pi]
=
b\nabla\sum_a\pi(a|s)
=
b\nabla1
=
0.
\]

The baseline can depend on state/prompt but should not depend on the sampled action in a way that introduces bias.

---

## Q3

Two rollouts have rewards \(1\) and \(0\). Compare raw rewards with baseline \(0.5\).

### Answer

Raw reward:
- success gets positive update,
- failure gets no direct update.

With baseline \(0.5\):
- success gets advantage \(+0.5\),
- failure gets advantage \(-0.5\).

The baseline turns the signal into “better or worse than expected.”

---

## Q4

Distinguish \(\pi_\theta\), \(\pi_{\text{old}}\), and \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\).

### Answer

- \(\pi_\theta\): current trainable policy.
- \(\pi_{\text{old}}\): rollout-generating policy used in importance ratios.
- \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\): frozen behavioral anchor used for KL regularization.

Confusing old and reference policy confuses local update correction with long-term drift control.

---

## Q5

Why does PPO clipping not guarantee the final ratio stays in \([1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\)?

### Answer

The objective is clipped, not the policy parameters.

Shared neural-network updates from other tokens/examples can still push a ratio outside the interval.

Clipping only removes extra direct incentive beyond the bound.

---

## Q6

GRPO improves pass@1, entropy collapses, pass@64 coverage does not improve.

What mechanism would you propose?

### Answer

Likely sequence:

1. already-accessible successful modes are sampled,
2. they receive positive advantage,
3. their probability rises,
4. they are sampled even more often,
5. positive feedback concentrates the policy,
6. entropy falls,
7. high-\(k\) coverage changes little because few new problems become accessible.

This is a strong sharpening/exploitation signature.

---

# Block 4 Summary

The essential progression is

\[
\boxed{
\text{SFT: imitate}
\rightarrow
\text{DPO: rank}
\rightarrow
\text{policy gradient: generate, evaluate, reinforce}.
}
\]

Core equations:

\[
J=E[R]
\]

\[
\nabla J=E[R\nabla\log\pi]
\]

\[
A=R-b
\]

\[
r=\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{old}}
\]

\[
L_{\text{PPO}}
=
\min(rA,\operatorname{clip}(r)A).
\]

The key research tension is exploration versus exploitation.
